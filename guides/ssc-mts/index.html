Exam overview and where the cycle stands

The Staff Selection Commission (SSC) runs one combined exam for two kinds of posts: Multi-Tasking (Non-Technical) Staff, called MTS, and Havaldar in CBIC (customs and GST) and CBN (narcotics). Both need only Class 10. Both are Group C posts at Pay Level 1.

The last completed cycle is the MTS and Havaldar Examination 2025. The notice came out on 26 June 2025 with 7,948 posts. Applications closed on 24 July 2025. The computer based exam (CBE) started on 4 February 2026. The result was declared on 3 August 2026 and Havaldar PET/PST was scheduled for 24-31 August 2026.

For the 2026 cycle, the SSC tentative calendar for 2026-27 listed the notice for June 2026 and the exam for September-November 2026. SSC treats calendar dates as tentative. We could not confirm a final 2026 notice, vacancy count or exam date as of October 2026, so check the notice board on ssc.gov.in and our exam calendar before you plan.

Upper age relaxation follows central rules: 3 years for OBC (NCL), 5 years for SC/ST and 10 years for PwBD (more if combined with a reserved category). Use the age calculator with the cut-off date in the new notice, and read our guide on age relaxation rules.

Education: Matriculation (Class 10) from a recognised board, passed by the closing date. The fee in recent cycles was Rs 100. Women, SC, ST, PwBD and eligible ex-servicemen do not pay.

If you are 25 or under, you are considered for both age groups. If you are 26 or 27, you compete only for the 18-27 MTS posts and CBIC Havaldar. That second pool is small, so the cut-off can move sharply.

Session-wise exam pattern

There is one computer based exam with two sessions on the same day. Both sessions are compulsory. Session-I closes on its own after 45 minutes and Session-II opens straight after.

Each question carries 3 marks. Total: 90 questions and 270 marks. Candidates eligible for a scribe get 60 minutes per session. Questions are at Class 10 level. There is no descriptive paper in the current scheme.

Havaldar candidates sit the same CBE. Those shortlisted then take the PET/PST. MTS candidates have no physical test.

How marks are actually decided

Most aspirants treat the two sessions as one paper. They are not used the same way.

  • Session-I has no negative marking. Attempt all 40 questions. A blank answer earns nothing, a guess can earn 3.
  • Session-II costs 1 mark per wrong answer against 3 for a right one. A guess between two options is still worth taking. A blind guess among four is close to break-even, so skip it.
  • Minimum qualifying marks reported for the 2023 cycle were 30% for UR, 25% for OBC and EWS, and 20% for others.
  • Merit: in the 2023 cycle the merit list was drawn on Session-II marks. Session-I acted as a gate and a tie-breaker. Read the marking paragraph of your own notice, since SSC can change this.
  • Tie-break order reported for 2023: higher General Awareness marks in Session-II, then higher Session-I total, then older candidate, then alphabetical order.
  • Normalisation: the exam runs over many shifts, so raw marks are normalised before cut-offs are applied. Compare normalised scores, not raw answer-key totals, when you guess your chances.

Cut-offs are state-wise, category-wise and age-group-wise. A score that clears Bihar may fall short in Delhi, which had the largest share of MTS posts in 2025.

PET and PST for Havaldar

Only Havaldar candidates shortlisted from the CBE take these. Both are qualifying, so passing them adds no marks.

Relaxation: height by 5 cm for men and 2.5 cm for women, and women's weight by 2 kg, for Garhwalis, Assamese, Gorkhas and Scheduled Tribes. It is a walk, not a run. People fail it by starting too fast and slowing down in the last laps. Practise a steady pace of under 9 minutes 20 seconds per km for men.

The PET/PST is held by the CBIC or CBN field offices, not SSC. Carry the call letter, photo ID and the documents listed in it.

Salary and Pay Level

MTS and Havaldar are both at Pay Level 1 of the 7th CPC matrix (Rs 18,000-56,900). DA was raised to 60% of basic pay from 1 January 2026. The July 2026 instalment had not been confirmed when we checked. HRA is 30%, 20% or 10% of basic by city class.

MTS or Havaldar: which to opt for

  • Opt for both if you meet the physical standards. Opting for both gives you two chances from one exam, and the only extra step is the PET/PST.
  • If you are below the PST height and do not fall in a relaxed group, opt for MTS only. You save a trip and a wasted PET slot.
  • Check the state-wise vacancy table in the notice before choosing your exam centre region. Cut-offs are drawn state-wise, so a state with very few posts in your category can need a high score.

Common mistakes that cost candidates

  • Choosing the wrong age group mentally. If you are 26, prepare for the 18-27 pool cut-off, which can be higher than the 18-25 one.
  • Spending Session-I time on hard arithmetic. Every question is worth the same 3 marks. Do reasoning first if it is faster for you.
  • Guessing wildly in General Awareness. Session-II merit decides your rank, and minus 1 adds up fast.
  • Photo and signature mismatch, or a name spelt differently from the Class 10 certificate. These cause rejection at document stage.
  • Missing the exam slot selection window. In the 2025 cycle SSC opened slot selection on 16-25 January 2026. Candidates who skip it get a slot assigned.
  • Claiming OBC or EWS with a certificate dated after the cut-off. Keep a certificate valid on the notice dates.

A 90-day plan

  1. Days 1-30: Class 9-10 arithmetic (percentage, ratio, time and work, average, simple interest) and reasoning basics. One 40-question Session-I set every second day.
  2. Days 31-60: English grammar rules, synonyms, error spotting and one reading passage daily. Static GK: history, polity, geography, basic science.
  3. Days 61-80: Full mocks with both sessions back to back, timed at 45 + 45 minutes. Review every wrong Session-II answer. Use our mock tests and previous year papers.
  4. Days 81-90: Six months of current affairs, revision notes, and for Havaldar a 1.6 km walk every morning.

Documents to keep ready

  • Class 10 marksheet and certificate (date of birth proof)
  • Category certificate (SC, ST, OBC-NCL or EWS) in the central government format
  • PwBD certificate, if claiming
  • Ex-servicemen discharge papers, if claiming
  • Aadhaar or another photo ID matching the one used in the form
  • Recent passport photos with a white background, and the printed application

Frequently asked questions

Is there negative marking in SSC MTS?

Session-I (maths and reasoning) has no negative marking. Session-II (general awareness and English) deducts 1 mark for each wrong answer, against 3 marks for a correct one.

Do MTS candidates have to take a physical test?

No. PET and PST apply only to candidates shortlisted for Havaldar posts in CBIC and CBN.

What is the age limit for SSC MTS?

In the 2025 cycle MTS posts were in two groups, 18-25 and 18-27 years. CBN Havaldar was 18-25 and CBIC Havaldar 18-27, with relaxation for reserved categories.

Has the SSC MTS 2026 notice been released?

The SSC tentative calendar listed it for June 2026 with the exam in September-November 2026. We could not confirm the final notice as of October 2026, so check ssc.gov.in.

What is the salary of SSC MTS?

MTS and Havaldar are at Pay Level 1 with Rs 18,000 basic pay. With DA at 60% and HRA, the total before deductions is about Rs 30,600-34,200 depending on city class.

Can I apply for both MTS and Havaldar?

Yes. One application covers both, and you can opt for Havaldar along with MTS if you meet the physical standards.
