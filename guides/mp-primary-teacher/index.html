MP Primary Teacher (Varg 3): MPTET, PSTST Pattern, Eligibility, Pay

The two-step route

Since 2018, MP fills primary teacher posts in government schools through two separate exams, both run by the Madhya Pradesh Employees Selection Board (MPESB):

  1. MPTET Varg 3 (Prathmik Shikshak Patrata Pariksha): an eligibility test. Passing it does not give a job. It only makes you eligible for step 2.
  2. Prathmik Shikshak Chayan Pariksha (PSTST): the actual selection test for vacant posts. Merit in this exam decides who is appointed.

The posts are in the School Education Department and the Tribal Affairs Department schools, for Classes 1-5. Many candidates confuse the two exams and stop after the eligibility test. Do not. You must clear both.

The gap between the two tests can be long. The 2024 eligibility test was held in November 2024, and the selection test based on it came only in October 2025. Use that time to prepare for the second exam, because its syllabus is the same and only the competition is tougher.

Eligibility: D.El.Ed, B.El.Ed and the B.Ed question

Under Rule 8 and Schedule III of the MP school teacher recruitment rules 2018, you need one of these:

  • Class 12 with at least 50% plus a 2-year D.El.Ed (or equivalent elementary education diploma).
  • Class 12 with at least 45% plus a 2-year D.El.Ed under the NCTE 2002 regulations.
  • Class 12 with at least 50% plus a 4-year B.El.Ed.
  • Class 12 with at least 50% plus a 2-year diploma in special education.
  • Graduation plus a 2-year D.El.Ed or equivalent.

SC, ST, OBC and PwD candidates get a 5% relaxation in the minimum marks. For the Science teacher posts in 2025, Class 12 had to be in the Science stream.

B.Ed holders: a B.Ed is not on this list. After the Supreme Court's August 2023 ruling in the Devesh Sharma case, B.Ed is not accepted for primary (Class 1-5) teacher posts. A B.Ed holder should look at the Varg 2 (middle school) route instead.

You also need the MPTET Varg 3 qualifying certificate of 2020 or 2024, as the PSTST 2025 rule book specified. Check your profile on the eligibility checker.

Exam pattern

Both the eligibility test and the selection test use the primary-level TET structure: 150 objective questions of 1 mark each, with no negative marking, in five sections.

For MPTET Varg 3, the pass mark is 60% for unreserved candidates and 50% for MP domicile SC, ST, OBC and EWS candidates. The same 90/150 and 75/150 thresholds were reported for the PSTST 2025 result. The PSTST 2025 shifts were reported as 10:30 am-12:30 pm and 3:00-5:00 pm; check the rule book for the exact duration in the next cycle.

Syllabus in brief

  • Child Development and Pedagogy: growth and development, Piaget, Vygotsky, Kohlberg, Pavlov, Thorndike, inclusive education, learning difficulties, assessment, motivation.
  • Language I and II: unseen passages, grammar, vocabulary, language teaching methods, the four skills (listening, speaking, reading, writing).
  • Mathematics: number system, fractions, geometry, measurement, data handling, patterns and maths pedagogy.
  • EVS: family, plants, animals, food, water, shelter, travel, environment and EVS pedagogy.

About a third of the maths and EVS sections are pedagogy questions, not content. Candidates with a science degree often lose marks there. See our syllabus and previous papers, including the 2020 and 2024 MPTET papers.

How selection and school allotment work

  1. PSTST written exam: a merit list is made from the scores, separately for each post code (department and subject group).
  2. Online choice filling: the School Education and Tribal Affairs departments open portals where you list schools or districts in order of preference.
  3. Document verification: at the district, of marksheets, D.El.Ed or B.El.Ed, MPTET certificate, domicile and category certificates.
  4. Medical and appointment.

Which department to prefer: Tribal Affairs schools are often in remote blocks of districts like Jhabua, Alirajpur, Dindori and Mandla. Fewer candidates list them first, so they can open up for a lower rank, but the posting may be far from home for years. If you score in the top band, you can aim for School Education schools near towns. If your score is borderline, listing more Tribal Affairs schools widens your chance of any selection. Fill every preference slot you are willing to join; a short list is the most common reason a good score ends with no allotment.

How your merit position is really decided

The MPTET score only opens the door. Your rank comes from the PSTST score, compared within your category and post code. Since the PSTST runs in shifts, compare your score with others of your post code, not with friends who applied for Science posts. When the result shows two lists, as with the July 2026 revised result, always check the later one: candidates were added after the late D.El.Ed uploads, which can shift ranks by a few places.

Common mistakes

  • Applying for PSTST with a B.Ed in place of D.El.Ed or B.El.Ed.
  • Treating the MPTET pass as a job offer and not applying for PSTST.
  • Applying with a D.El.Ed whose final result is not out. In 2025 a separate upload window had to be opened for this.
  • Science posts: applying without Science in Class 12.
  • Category claim without an MP-issued certificate, which also loses the 50% MPTET pass mark.
  • Wrong post code or department preference, which cannot be changed after the correction window.

Documents to keep ready: Class 10 and 12 marksheets, D.El.Ed or B.El.Ed marksheets and certificate, MPTET Varg 3 scorecard, MP domicile, caste or EWS certificate, PwD certificate if any, Aadhaar and photos.

A 90-day plan for the next round

Days 1-30: Child Development and Pedagogy every day, since it carries 30 marks and decides both tests. Make one-page notes on each theorist, with one example question for each, and revise them every Sunday.

Days 31-60: Class 1-5 NCERT and MP board maths and EVS books, with their pedagogy chapters. Do one language section a day, alternating Hindi and English so neither falls behind.

Days 61-90: solve the 2020 and 2024 MPTET papers and full 150-question mock tests. Your target is 110 or more, well above the 90 pass mark, because selection merit is set by the top scorers, not by the pass line. If you also plan to teach in central schools, the CTET guide uses the same syllabus.

Frequently asked questions

Is MPTET Varg 3 enough to get a teacher job?

No. It is only an eligibility test. You must also clear the Prathmik Shikshak Chayan Pariksha (PSTST) conducted by MPESB to be selected.

Can B.Ed holders become primary teachers in MP?

No. After the Supreme Court's 2023 ruling, B.Ed is not accepted for Class 1-5 posts. You need D.El.Ed, B.El.Ed or a special education diploma.

What is the passing mark in MPTET Varg 3?

60% for unreserved candidates and 50% for MP domicile SC, ST, OBC and EWS candidates.

Is there a new primary teacher vacancy in MP in 2026?

No new MPTET Varg 3 or PSTST notice has been announced as of October 2026. The PSTST 2025 revised result came on 14 July 2026.

What is the age limit for MP primary teacher?

21 to 40 years for unreserved and EWS men, and up to 45 years for MP SC, ST, OBC, PwD candidates and women, as per PSTST 2025.

What is the salary of a primary teacher in MP?

The notice lists a starting basic of Rs 25,300 plus DA. New recruits get 70%, 80% and 90% of it in the three probation years.
