What CTET is and who needs it

The Central Teacher Eligibility Test (CTET) is conducted by the Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE). It is an eligibility test, not a recruitment exam. Clearing it does not give you a job. It makes you eligible to apply for teacher posts in central government schools such as Kendriya Vidyalayas (KVS) and Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalayas (NVS), and in schools under the Union Territories. Many private schools and some states also accept the CTET certificate.

The demand is huge. In the February 2026 session, 26,49,129 candidates registered and 23,24,625 appeared, as per the CBSE result notice. Only 5,97,061 qualified, which is 25.68 percent. So treat CTET as a serious exam, not a formality.

The official website is ctet.nic.in. All notices, the Information Bulletin, admit cards and results are published there.

Paper 1 vs Paper 2: which one to write

CTET has two papers. You can write one or both in the same session.

  • Paper 1 is for teachers of Classes 1 to 5 (Primary Teacher, PRT).
  • Paper 2 is for teachers of Classes 6 to 8 (Trained Graduate Teacher level for the upper primary stage).

Paper 2 has two options for the main subject section: Mathematics and Science, or Social Studies/Social Science. Teachers of other subjects can choose either one.

Each paper is 2 hours 30 minutes. Each question carries 1 mark and there is no negative marking. The September 2026 bulletin says the 22nd edition will be held offline on OMR sheets, with the paper available in 27 languages.

Which to write: In the February 2026 session, 33.69 percent passed Paper 1 but only 18.56 percent passed Paper 2. If you have D.El.Ed, write Paper 1. If you have B.Ed, you are eligible for Paper 2, and you should also check whether your target employer accepts B.Ed for primary posts under current rules before relying on Paper 1. Writing both costs more but gives you two chances in one sitting.

Eligibility in short

The minimum age is 17 years and there is no upper age limit for CTET. Candidates in the final year of a recognised teacher training course can also apply.

  • Paper 1: Senior Secondary (Class 12) with the required percentage plus a 2-year D.El.Ed, or a 4-year B.El.Ed, or a 2-year Diploma in Education (Special Education). The required percentage is 45 or 50 percent depending on the route, as listed in the bulletin.
  • Paper 2: Graduation plus a 2-year D.El.Ed or 1-year B.Ed, or Class 12 plus a 4-year B.El.Ed or B.A.Ed/B.Sc.Ed, or graduation with 50 percent plus B.Ed (Special Education).

Read the exact percentage rule for your route in the current Information Bulletin. Many candidates assume 45 percent works for every route and get stuck at document verification later, when an employer checks the original marksheets.

Qualifying marks by category

The CTET bulletin sets 60 percent as the pass mark. It also says that school managements (government, local bodies, aided and unaided) may give concessions to reserved categories as per their own policies. In practice, central government employers such as KVS and NVS accept 55 percent for reserved categories.

PwD55%82.5

Two practical points. First, CBSE prints the result for everyone; whether 83 is enough is decided by the recruiting body, not by CBSE. Second, there is no normalisation in CTET because each paper is a single offline sitting. Your raw score is your score, so every mark from the answer key challenge matters.

Certificate validity: lifetime

Until 2021, a TET certificate was valid for 7 years. On 3 June 2021, the Ministry of Education announced that TET certificates will be valid for life. The change applies retrospectively to certificates issued from 2011 onwards. State governments and Union Territories were asked to revalidate or reissue certificates whose 7-year period had expired.

So if you cleared CTET in 2014, your certificate is still valid. You do not need to write CTET again just to keep eligibility. Many candidates still write it again to improve marks, because some employers use CTET marks in shortlisting or tie-breaking. Check the specific notification for this.

Your marksheet and eligibility certificate are uploaded to DigiLocker. Download them using the mobile number you gave in the application form and keep a printed copy.

How CTET is used in KVS and NVS hiring

For KVS and NVS, CTET is a must-have qualification for teacher posts up to Class 8. It is checked at the time of applying and again at document verification.

  • PRT (Primary Teacher): CTET Paper 1 pass is required. Pay Level 6 (starting basic Rs 35,400).
  • TGT (Trained Graduate Teacher): CTET Paper 2 pass is required. Pay Level 7 (starting basic Rs 44,900).
  • PGT (Post Graduate Teacher): CTET is not required. Pay Level 8.

The last combined drive was notified by CBSE in November 2025 for 14,967 teaching and non-teaching posts in KVS and NVS. This included 3,365 PRT posts in KVS and 5,772 TGT posts across KVS and NVS. The selection used a written exam followed by an interview for teaching posts. Your CTET score qualifies you to apply, while the KVS/NVS exam decides selection.

So the order is simple: clear CTET first, then prepare for the KVS/NVS exam. A CTET pass after the KVS application last date usually does not count. Write the CTET session that ends before the next KVS notice.

For state jobs, the state TET or the state rules decide. For example, Madhya Pradesh runs its own teacher eligibility and selection tests. See our MP primary teacher guide.

Common mistakes that cost candidates

  • Wrong Language I and Language II choice. You pick them in the form and cannot change them in the hall. Language I is the medium you teach in. Choose the language you read fastest, usually Hindi for MP and UP candidates.
  • Wrong paper option in Paper 2. A Science B.Ed holder who picks Social Studies has to study an unfamiliar 60-mark section.
  • Photo and signature mismatch. Use a recent photo. A blurry upload is a common reason for problems at the centre.
  • Skipping the answer key challenge. With no negative marking and a hard 90-mark line, a candidate on 88 or 89 should check the provisional key carefully. CBSE charges a fee per challenge, which is refunded if your challenge is accepted.
  • Leaving OMR bubbles blank. There is no negative marking, so never leave a question unanswered.

A 60-day plan for the December 2026 paper

  1. Days 1-20: Child Development and Pedagogy every day. It is common to both papers and decides most results. Read NCERT-based theory: Piaget, Vygotsky, Kohlberg, inclusive education, assessment (CCE).
  2. Days 21-40: Your subject section (Maths and EVS for Paper 1, or Maths-Science / Social Studies for Paper 2) from NCERT Class 6-8 books, plus the pedagogy questions of that subject.
  3. Days 41-50: Language I and II. Practise unseen passages and grammar pedagogy.
  4. Days 51-60: One full paper every second day under 150-minute timing. Use our previous year papers and mock tests.

Target 100 or above in mocks. That gives you a margin over 90.

Frequently asked questions

When is the next CTET exam?

The 22nd edition of CTET is scheduled for 12 and 13 December 2026 after being moved from 6 September 2026. The 2027 session notification has not been announced as of October 2026.

What are the CTET qualifying marks?

The bulletin sets 60 percent, which is 90 out of 150. Employers such as KVS and NVS accept 55 percent for SC, ST, OBC and PwD candidates.

Is the CTET certificate valid for life?

Yes. The Ministry of Education made TET certificates valid for life in June 2021, with effect from certificates issued in 2011 onwards.

Is there negative marking in CTET?

No. Each question carries 1 mark and wrong answers are not penalised, so attempt every question.

Can I write both Paper 1 and Paper 2?

Yes, if you meet the eligibility for both. The fee is higher for both papers, and the two papers are held in different shifts.

Is CTET required for KVS PGT?

No. CTET is needed for PRT (Paper 1) and TGT (Paper 2) posts. PGT posts do not require CTET.

Is there an age limit for CTET?

The minimum age is 17 years. There is no upper age limit for writing CTET, though the recruiting school or body will have its own age limit.
