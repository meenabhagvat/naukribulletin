SSC CGL 2026 — exam overview

The Combined Graduate Level (CGL) Examination is conducted every year by the Staff Selection Commission (SSC) to recruit graduates into Group B and Group C posts across central government ministries, departments and organisations — including the Income Tax Department, Customs & Central Excise, CBI, the Ministry of External Affairs and the offices of the CAG. With over 20 lakh applicants each year, it is one of India's most competitive government exams.

What's new in SSC CGL 2026

Two changes stand out this cycle, and both affect how you should prepare:

  • Sectional timing in Tier 1 and Tier 2. Each section now has its own time limit, so you can no longer borrow time from an easy section to spend on a hard one. Speed in every individual section matters more than before.
  • Assistant Audit Officer / Assistant Accounts Officer (AAO) is back. After a gap, this prestigious Pay Level 8 post has been re-introduced, which is a strong draw for commerce and finance graduates.

The salary, broad exam pattern and overall selection flow remain the same as recent years.

Vacancies & popular posts

SSC has announced 12,256 tentative vacancies across roughly 40 posts. Some of the most sought-after roles are:

  • Assistant Section Officer (ASO) — in the CSS, Ministry of External Affairs, Intelligence Bureau, etc.
  • Income Tax Inspector and Central Excise / GST Inspector — CBDT and CBIC.
  • Assistant Audit Officer / Assistant Accounts Officer (AAO) — under the CAG (Pay Level 8).
  • Assistant Enforcement Officer (AEO) — Directorate of Enforcement.
  • Auditor, Accountant, Tax Assistant and Junior Statistical Officer (JSO).

The final vacancy count and category-wise (UR / OBC / SC / ST / EWS) split are confirmed in the official notification PDF.

Eligibility & age limit

Educational qualification

A bachelor's degree in any discipline from a recognised university is the minimum requirement for most posts. Final-year students can apply but must hold the degree by the cut-off date. Certain posts have extra requirements:

  • JSO (Junior Statistical Officer): degree with Statistics as a subject (or Mathematics in Class 12 at 60%).
  • AAO: bachelor's degree; a CA / CMA / CS / MBA (Finance) or M.Com is desirable.

Age limit

The age limit is generally 18–32 years depending on the post, calculated as on 1 August 2026. Reserved categories get relaxation as per government norms (typically +3 years for OBC, +5 for SC/ST, +10 for PwBD, with further relaxation for ex-servicemen). Check the post-wise age band in the notification.

Nationality

You must be a citizen of India (or fall under the other eligible categories specified by the Commission, such as certain persons of Indian origin who have migrated to India with the intention of permanent settlement).

Application & fee

Applications are accepted online only at ssc.gov.in, after completing the One-Time Registration (OTR).

  • Application fee: ₹100 for General and OBC candidates.
  • Fee exemption: Women, SC, ST, PwBD and eligible ex-servicemen pay no fee.

Choose your Tier 1 and Tier 2 exam-centre cities carefully — centre-change requests are usually not entertained later.

Selection process

Selection happens in two computer-based stages, with no interview and no descriptive paper:

  1. Tier 1 — Qualifying. Screens candidates for Tier 2. Marks are not added to the final merit.
  2. Tier 2 — Merit-deciding. Your Tier 2 score determines the final rank and which post you are allotted, followed by document verification.

Because Tier 2 decides everything, your real goal is to comfortably clear Tier 1 and then maximise Tier 2.

SSC CGL Tier 2 exam pattern

Tier 2 has up to three papers; Paper 1 is compulsory for all, while Paper 2 (for JSO) and Paper 3 (for AAO) are post-specific.

Paper 1 is conducted in sections/modules with their own timing; the MCQ sections carry 1 mark negative marking per wrong answer. The Computer Knowledge module and DEST are qualifying in nature for relevant posts.

Syllabus overview

The four core areas to master:

  • Quantitative Aptitude: number system, percentages, ratio & proportion, profit & loss, time-speed-distance, time & work, mensuration, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, data interpretation.
  • Reasoning & General Intelligence: analogies, classification, series, coding-decoding, syllogism, blood relations, direction sense, puzzles, non-verbal reasoning.
  • English: reading comprehension, error spotting, sentence improvement, fill-ups, synonyms/antonyms, idioms, cloze test, para-jumbles.
  • General Awareness: static GK (history, geography, polity, economics), general science, and current affairs of the last 6–12 months.

A practical preparation strategy

You don't need 16 hours a day — you need consistent, measured practice. A workable plan:

  1. Build the base (weeks 1–4): finish the high-weight Quant and Reasoning chapters; read one newspaper or our daily current affairs every day.
  2. Practice with timing (ongoing): because 2026 has sectional timing, practise each section against the clock, not just full mocks.
  3. Mock tests (weekly → daily): take a full Tier-1 mock, then analyse every wrong answer. Your accuracy and time-per-question matter more than raw attempts.
  4. Revise GA relentlessly: General Awareness is the fastest-scoring section — short, factual, no calculation.

Frequently asked questions

Is the SSC CGL 2026 notification released?

Yes. SSC released the SSC CGL 2026 notification on 21 May 2026 at ssc.gov.in for 12,256 tentative vacancies. The online application window has closed.

When is the SSC CGL 2026 exam date?

The Tier 1 exam is tentatively scheduled for August–September 2026 and Tier 2 for December 2026. Confirmed dates are announced on ssc.gov.in.

How many vacancies are there?

12,256 tentative vacancies across about 40 Group B and Group C posts. SSC may revise the figure during the recruitment.

What is the eligibility and age limit?

A bachelor's degree from a recognised university; some posts need specific subjects. Age is generally 18–32 years (post-wise) as on 1 August 2026, with relaxations for reserved categories.

What's new in 2026?

Sectional timing has been introduced in both Tier 1 and Tier 2, and the Assistant Audit Officer (AAO) post has been re-introduced.

Is there an interview?

No. Selection is through Tier 1 (qualifying) and Tier 2 (merit-deciding) only, followed by document verification. There is no interview or descriptive paper.

What is the Tier 1 exam pattern?

Four sections — Reasoning, General Awareness, Quantitative Aptitude and English — with 100 questions for 200 marks in 60 minutes, and 0.5 mark negative marking per wrong answer.

What salary can I expect?

It depends on the post and pay level — for example, ASO/Inspector posts (Level 7) start around ₹44,900 basic, and AAO (Level 8) carries a ₹47,600–₹1,51,100 scale, plus DA, HRA and other allowances.
