What "salary" means in a government job

Every central government job advertisement mentions a Pay Level, for example "Pay Level 4 (Rs 25,500-81,100)". That number is only the basic pay. What reaches your bank account each month is basic pay plus Dearness Allowance (DA), House Rent Allowance (HRA) and Transport Allowance (TA), minus pension contribution, insurance and income tax.

This page explains each part using the 7th Central Pay Commission (7th CPC) rules that still apply in October 2026. It covers Pay Levels 1-10, which include almost every post filled through SSC, RRB, IBPS-style central recruitment and most Group B and C jobs. State government jobs in Madhya Pradesh use a separate state pay matrix, so the numbers below apply to central posts only.

If you want your own figure, use the salary calculator after reading how the parts fit together.

7th CPC Pay Matrix: Levels 1-10 starting basic pay

The pay matrix replaced the old pay band plus grade pay system from 1 January 2016. Each Pay Level has a column of cells. You join at the first cell and move one cell up every year on 1 January or 1 July, which is roughly a 3% increment.

Dearness Allowance: current rate in October 2026

DA is a percentage of basic pay that the Centre revises twice a year, for January and July, based on the All India Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers (AICPI-IW).

  • Current rate: 60% of basic pay, effective 1 January 2026. The Union Cabinet approved this 2 percentage point hike on 18 April 2026, taking DA from 58% to 60%.
  • July 2026 instalment: not announced as of 16 October 2026. Calculations from published AICPI data point to 63%, but there is no Cabinet decision or Department of Expenditure order yet. When it is approved, it will apply from 1 July 2026 with arrears.

The examples on this page use 60% because that is the rate actually being paid. If DA moves to 63%, add 3% of basic pay to the DA line and recalculate the pension deduction.

HRA and Transport Allowance

House Rent Allowance

HRA depends on the class of the city where you are posted. Under the Department of Expenditure order of July 2017, the rates were 24%, 16% and 8%, with automatic increases when DA crossed 25% and again at 50%. Since DA reached 50% from 1 January 2024, the rates are:

HRA is not paid if you live in government quarters. In that case a licence fee is deducted instead.

Transport Allowance

TA is a fixed amount plus DA on that amount. It is higher in 19 listed "higher TA cities". In Madhya Pradesh only Indore is on that list. Bhopal, despite being a Y city for HRA, gets the ordinary rate.

Deductions: NPS or UPS, CGEGIS and income tax

  • NPS or UPS: Anyone joining central service after 1 January 2004 is covered by the National Pension System. The employee contributes 10% of (basic + DA) every month. The Unified Pension Scheme (UPS), available from 1 April 2025 as an option within NPS, also takes 10% from the employee. The government's share (14% under NPS, 18.5% under UPS) is added to your account and does not reduce your in-hand pay.
  • CGEGIS: The Central Government Employees Group Insurance Scheme deducts a small fixed amount: Rs 30 for most Group C posts, Rs 60 for Group B and Rs 120 for Group A.
  • Income tax: Budget 2026 kept the new tax regime slabs unchanged. With the Rs 75,000 standard deduction and the rebate up to Rs 12 lakh of taxable income, a salaried person with gross salary up to about Rs 12.75 lakh a year pays no income tax under the new regime. Most Level 1-7 freshers fall below this line.
  • Other: Some offices deduct GPF-style recoveries, society subscriptions, licence fee for quarters or professional tax where a state levies it. These vary by office.

Where candidates get the salary figure wrong

  1. Quoting the old DA. Many websites still show 50%, 53% or 55%. Check the date of the latest Cabinet decision on pib.gov.in.
  2. Using X-city HRA for every post. Your first posting may be in a Z town. For a Level 5 job, the gap between X and Z HRA is Rs 5,840 a month.
  3. Forgetting NPS. The 10% cut is on basic plus DA, so it grows every time DA rises.
  4. Counting the employer NPS share as salary. It is retirement savings, not monthly cash.
  5. Ignoring special allowances. Railway running staff, CAPF personnel and posts in the North East get allowances such as risk, ration or special duty allowance. These are post specific and are listed in the department's own orders.
  6. Comparing state and central jobs on basic pay alone. MP state DA and HRA rates differ from central rates, so compare in-hand pay, not the Level number.

8th Pay Commission: status as of October 2026

The 8th Central Pay Commission was constituted by a Department of Expenditure resolution dated 3 November 2025. It is chaired by Justice Ranjana Prakash Desai, with Prof. Pulak Ghosh as part-time member and Pankaj Jain as Member-Secretary. It has 18 months from constitution to report, which points to around May 2027, and it may send interim reports earlier.

As of September-October 2026 the Commission is holding consultations with unions and departments in different cities, including Mumbai meetings scheduled for 22-23 October 2026. It has not announced any fitment factor, new pay matrix or revised minimum pay. Figures such as "minimum pay Rs 51,000" circulating online are union demands or guesses, not decisions.

Pay revisions are normally applied from 1 January 2026, the date the 10-year cycle ends, with arrears paid after the government accepts the report. Until then, the 7th CPC matrix and DA instalments above continue.

Using pay level to choose between posts

When an exam like SSC CHSL or CGL asks you to rank post preferences, look at three things together: Pay Level, likely posting city and promotion path. A Level 4 post in a Delhi ministry pays more HRA and TA than a Level 4 post in a field office in a Z town, but the field office may offer quarters and lower living costs. A Level 2 clerical post with a clear departmental exam route to Level 4 can overtake a Level 4 post with a slow promotion ladder within 6-8 years.

For exam-wise detail, read the guides for SSC CHSL, SSC GD Constable and RRB Group D.

Frequently asked questions

What is the current DA rate for central government employees?

60% of basic pay from 1 January 2026, approved by the Cabinet on 18 April 2026. The July 2026 instalment has not been announced as of 16 October 2026.

Is Bhopal an X, Y or Z city for HRA?

Bhopal and Indore are Y class cities, so HRA is 20% of basic pay with a minimum of Rs 3,600. For Transport Allowance, only Indore is a higher TA city in Madhya Pradesh.

What is the in-hand salary at Pay Level 1?

At 18,000 basic in Bhopal: DA 10,800, HRA 3,600, TA 1,440 (900 + DA), gross about Rs 33,840. After NPS of Rs 2,880 and CGEGIS of Rs 30, in-hand is about Rs 30,930.

Does a new government employee pay income tax?

Under the new regime, salaried income up to about Rs 12.75 lakh a year has no tax after the standard deduction and rebate. Most freshers in Levels 1-7 fall below this.

When will the 8th Pay Commission salary be paid?

The Commission was constituted on 3 November 2025 with 18 months to report. No new pay matrix has been announced; any revision is expected to be applied from 1 January 2026 with arrears once the government accepts the report.

Is NPS deduction compulsory?

Yes for central employees joining after 1 January 2004. You can choose UPS instead, but the employee contribution is 10% of basic plus DA in both.
